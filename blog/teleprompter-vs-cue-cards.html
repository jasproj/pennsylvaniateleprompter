Teleprompter vs Cue Cards: Which is Better?

7 min read

Both teleprompters and cue cards help speakers deliver prepared content. But they serve different purposes and suit different situations. Here's how to choose for your Pennsylvania event or production.

Teleprompter Advantages

  • Direct eye contact with camera or audience
  • Can handle long, complex scripts
  • Easy real-time script changes
  • Consistent scroll speed
  • Professional, polished appearance

Cue Card Advantages

  • Lower cost for simple productions
  • No technology to fail
  • Can work without power
  • Visible gestures from card holder can cue timing

When to Use Teleprompters

  • On-camera work requiring eye contact
  • Long scripts (more than a few sentences)
  • Live events with multiple speakers
  • Professional productions with budgets
  • Any situation where looking polished matters

For corporate events at Philadelphia's Kimmel Center, broadcast segments on WPVI or KDKA, or political events at the Pennsylvania State Capitol in Harrisburg, teleprompters deliver the professional delivery audiences expect.

When Cue Cards Work

  • Brief bullet points only
  • Talk show style with visible personality
  • Very low budget productions
  • Outdoor locations without power

For informal events at smaller Pennsylvania venues, or quick promotional shoots in Center City Philadelphia, cue cards can work in a pinch—but they rarely impress sophisticated audiences or corporate clients.

The Verdict

For professional productions in Pennsylvania, teleprompters are almost always the better choice. The eye contact advantage alone—being able to look directly at your audience while delivering scripted content—is transformative.

Whether you're filming at the Philadelphia Studio or producing a live event at the David L. Lawrence Convention Center in Pittsburgh, professional teleprompter services elevate your production.
